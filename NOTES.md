# Notes

The judge's brief, in order: the machine itself is speaking; it uses the machine's own world (slot, buttons, coils, snacks, hum, exact change); the laugh lands by the last few words; short and speakable; charming, not creepy.

Candidates considered:
1. "You had me at B4." Short, but thin.
2. "I don't usually give out my number, but it's B7." Cute, small laugh.
3. "I've been humming since you walked in. Okay, I always hum, but this time I mean it." Warm, a bit long.
4. "Is it warm in here, or did my compressor just kick on?" Machine-ish, mild.
5. "For you, I'd drop anything. Fair warning: it might get stuck halfway." First draft: good punch, but a person could say the first half.
6. "You don't need exact change. You're exactly the change I needed." Classic pickup register, punny.
7. "My coils have been turning all day just thinking about you." Flat ending.
8. "Press B4 and I'll fall for you instantly. Well, mostly. A little part of me always gets stuck halfway." **Chosen.**

Why #8: "Press B4" makes the machine the speaker from the first words (criterion 1), "fall for you" is the pickup cliche taken literally as a dropping snack (criterion 2), and the stuck-halfway snack everyone has seen is the punch in the last words (criterion 3). It is self-deprecating and sweet, never creepy. 102 characters.

Revisions: draft #5 was reworked so the button code puts the machine in the line at once; then "A little part of me" softened the turn and a 1-200 character guard was added.
